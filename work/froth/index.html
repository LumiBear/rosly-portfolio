Website redesign for a campus café

Froth

A three-day redesign of Froth Coffee & Dessert Bar’s website, built from an in-person site visit and a staff interview, that swaps walls of text for a warm, visual, community-first experience.

01Overview

Three days, three pages, one café.

The brief: redesign a local business’s landing page plus two subpages in three days. I chose Froth Coffee & Dessert Bar, a popular café near the University of North Texas, because its space felt modern and lively while its website felt like neither.

Timeline
3 days
Role
UX/UI designer
Deliverables
Landing page, menu page, Work With Us page

02Site visit

I started by sitting in the café.

Before opening Figma, I spent time at Froth watching who came in and how they used the space. Most visitors were UNT students on their own or on study dates. Even the groups were studying. The room was quiet apart from pop music in the background.

The interior was modern and geometric, with a white base, orange accents, and touches of gold. The orange stood out immediately as the brand’s signature. I turned these notes into an attribute map that guided every visual decision afterward.

03Stakeholder interview

Cozy, warm, studious.

I interviewed a Froth employee with a short set of questions: who comes in, who the ideal customer is, how strong their social presence is, what keeps people coming back, what makes the coffee special, and how the place makes them feel. I closed by asking them to describe Froth in three words.

“Cozy, warm, studious.”

Froth employee, describing the café in three words

Those three words captured the café’s double life as a comfortable hangout and a productive study spot. The interview also surfaced how much Froth invests in fundraisers and local events, a differentiator the old website barely mentioned.

04Site audit

A designer’s worst fear: a wall of text.

  • About six dense paragraphs on the homepage alone
  • One café photo across the entire site
  • Only three pages: home, fundraising, and contact
  • No menu and no visual showcase of the drinks or desserts

With room for one landing page and two subpages, I prioritized the menu, since food and drink are the heart of a café, and a Work With Us page to give Froth’s community programs the visibility they deserved.

05Mood & direction

A mood board, and an anti-mood board.

I built two boards: one for where the site should go and one for what to avoid. The target was modern, fresh, and a little trendy, made for college students, without tipping into corporate or dated. That meant dynamic layouts, soft glass details, rounded corners, and much less text.

06Palette & type

Keeping the orange, adding depth.

White and orange were iconic, but on their own they couldn’t carry a content-rich site. I kept the signature orange and built around it with warm earth tones, a rich accent for hierarchy, neutrals for breathing room, and the gold already present in the café.

07Final design

More than just a café.

The landing page leads with photography and a short brand statement, “Every sip tells a story,” then answers practical questions fast: where it is, when it’s open, and what makes it good.

Work With Us turns community into a feature. “Grow with Froth” introduces three clear pathways, and a dedicated fundraisers section with small, concrete incentives like “1 cappuccino = $4 earned” makes the café’s local impact easy to see.

08Reflection

Context matters.

Sitting in the café revealed a quiet, studious atmosphere I would never have guessed from the website, and it pushed the design toward a calmer, more focused direction. With only three days, I chose in-person observation and an interview over a broad competitive analysis, and that trade-off paid off.

With more time, I would test the pages with UNT students, build an interactive prototype to show the micro-interactions, add About and Events pages, and turn the components into a small design system.
