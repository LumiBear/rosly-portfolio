01   Overview

Roommates rarely fight about the schedule. They fight about work nobody saw, a dishwasher nobody can read, and the one person who ended up as the manager.

Most chore apps add another step at the exact moment people are least willing to take it. ChoreTap moves the check-off to where the work happens. A small NFC sticker on the trash bin, dishwasher or cleaning caddy logs the chore with one tap, credits the person who did it, and updates the whole house instantly.

In the MVP

  • Automatic rotation
  • Gentle reminders
  • Web fallback for guests
  • Undo for accidental taps

  • PaymentsMoney turns a favor into a debt.
  • PenaltiesNobody opens an app that fines them.

Plenty of chore apps lead with these. Leaving them out is a design decision I expect to defend in testing.

06 Inside the app

Every screen, and why each part is there.

Scroll through six moments. The numbers on the phone match the notes beside it.

01 · Setup

Place a sticker in under a minute.

Setup happens at the spot. You scan it, name it, and it is live.

  1. 1
    The drawing teaches the gestureA phone meets a sticker, so the first action explains the product. No tutorial.
  2. 2
    Chips before typingCommon spots are one tap. Typing is the fallback because typing is the step people skip.
  3. 3
    Named after the object“Kitchen trash lid” reads like a sentence when it shows up in the feed later.
  4. 4
    Three dots, three stepsYou see how long this takes before you commit to it.
02 · Today

What’s yours, and what’s happening at home.

The home screen answers two questions. What do I owe, and what did everyone else just do.

  1. 1
    A greeting, then the jobDate, apartment and first name come first, so the screen reads as personal before it reads as a to-do list.
  2. 2
    Everyone in one glanceStacked avatars show who lives here. Housemates exist in the product from the first second.
  3. 3
    Your turn, with a Tap shortcutEach chore carries its own Tap button for when the sticker is out of reach or not placed yet.
  4. 4
    Washer and dryer timersLive rings show what is running around the house, so nobody texts “is the dryer done?”
03 · The tap

One second, then it’s done.

The confirmation rewards the tap, shows what changed and leaves a way out.

  1. 1
    A clear, quick rewardThe checked star pulses and the headline says exactly what was logged. You never wonder if it worked.
  2. 2
    Rotation is automaticThe next person appears right away. Nobody has to remember whose turn it is, or be the one who asks.
  3. 3
    Undo for a few secondsA visible countdown ring makes a wrong tap cheap to fix, which keeps the log honest.
  4. 4
    Credit goes to a nameThe feed says who did the work and when. Effort becomes visible without anyone announcing it.
04 · Stickers

The kit, at a glance.

A short list that answers what is stuck where, and whether it is working.

  1. 1
    Progress without pressureA segmented bar shows how much of the kit is placed. It nudges you to finish without scolding.
  2. 2
    Icons match the roomColor-coded tiles echo the Today screen, so one look connects the list to the object.
  3. 3
    Each sticker states its gestureTap, long-press or start a load. The rule sits next to the object, so you learn it by looking.
  4. 4
    Two statuses only“Active” or “Place it.” A sticker either works or needs you.
05 · Balance

Fair share, framed gently.

The hardest screen to get right. It shows the month as a picture and never ranks anyone.

  1. 1
    Week or month, one tapPeople can zoom out, because one bad week should not read as a bad housemate.
  2. 2
    One plain verdict“Even” sits in the middle as the headline. If it is lopsided, it says so calmly.
  3. 3
    Colors, not numbersThe donut shows shares with no percentages and no rank order. It is the opposite of a leaderboard.
  4. 4
    Noticing invisible workThe yellow card surfaces effort nobody asked for and offers a thank-you in one tap.
06 · Guest page

A fridge tag that needs no app.

Anyone can tap it: a new roommate, a friend staying over, a parent visiting.

  1. 1
    It opens on the webThe address bar shows a plain link. Nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. 2
    One-tap copyThe Wi-Fi password copies with one button. Typing a long password is what makes guests ask.
  3. 3
    The questions guests always askWi-Fi, trash day and house rules sit in separate cards, so the answer is where you look first.
  4. 4
    A way to say hi“Say hi to the house” lets a guest be seen without becoming an account.

08 Design decisions

Three calls, and what each one costs.

01

Tap a sticker, don’t scan a code

Why

A tap works one-handed with no camera, and it feels closer to touching the chore itself.

The trade-off

Some older phones and a few cases block NFC, so every sticker has a manual Tap button as backup.

02

A first name, no account

Why

Housemates only need to know who tapped. Asking for more makes the first tap the hardest one.

The trade-off

No account means no sync across devices at first. A guest’s history stays on their phone.

03

No leaderboards, ever

Why

A ranking turns a shared home into a contest and punishes the person doing the invisible work.

The trade-off

Less to play with. The bet is that being noticed is a better reward than a score.
